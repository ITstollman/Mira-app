The mark

One M, four cuts

Same gel, same plate, same light from the upper left. The only thing that changes between cuts is where the sparkles sit — pick by how busy the surface underneath already is.

Never recolour the M, rotate it, redraw the sparkles, or lift it off its plate — the pink-white plate is part of the mark. On a gel ground, use the plate as-is; the gloss is allowed to sit on gloss.

Elements

Nine objects, poured not drawn

Mira has no icon font and no stroke set. Every glyph is the same candy gel as the mark — five layers, light from the upper left — which means they read as small objects sitting on the screen rather than symbols printed onto it. That is expensive, so there are only nine.

One per surface. They are objects, not a toolbar — never line three up in a row, never below 28pt where the rim collapses, never recoloured, never outlined. If a screen needs a tenth idea, it gets a word instead.

The recipe

Five layers, painted in order

Zoom into any stroke of the mark and the gloss resolves into five distinct things stacked on top of each other. Strip any one out and the surface stops reading as gel. Toggle them.

Buttons

Three weights, one shape

Every button is a pill. The gel is reserved for the single thing you most want tapped on a screen — put two rose gels side by side and both stop meaning anything.

Pale gel · secondary

Ghost · tertiary

Never glossy. Destructive and dismissive actions live here.

Surfaces

Flat ground, so the gel reads

The single most important rule in the system, and the easiest to break: nothing behind a gel object may be glossy. Backgrounds, cards, sheets and bars are flat fills with a 1px hairline. Gloss on gloss is mud.

Mira is light only. There is no dark theme and no @media (prefers-color-scheme) anywhere — the root pins .preferredColorScheme(.light) and the cream ground is brand, not a default that the phone gets to override.

Ground   #FFF9FB   flat, full-bleed, no gradient ever

Motion

It should feel like weight, not speed

Gel objects have mass. They settle. Hover lifts 1px, press sinks 1px and saturates — the same way real gloss darkens when you push on it.

Tap        160ms  cubic-bezier(.22,.9,.3,1)   translateY ±1px + saturate
Swap       spring(response .45, damping .72)   garment change, size change
Sheet      spring(response .38, damping .84)   present / dismiss
Sheet→sheet 350ms delay before the second      SwiftUI drops it otherwise
Haptic     .light  select   ·   .medium  keep / love   ·   .heavy  blocked
Where gloss goes

The whole system in two columns

Gloss is a budget. Spend it on the thing you want touched.

Glossy

  • The mark, at every size — always on its plate, never cut out
  • The primary action on a screen — one only
  • The selected chip, size, plan or swatch
  • The shutter and the love puck
  • Badges that sell something (Most popular)

Flat

  • Every background, bar, sheet and card
  • Body copy, labels, prices, counters
  • Unselected anything
  • Destructive and dismissive buttons
  • Icons and hairlines
  • The camera feed and the garment overlay
